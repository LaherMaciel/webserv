topic 04 — the constraint that shapes everything

Why poll() exists

One process. One thread. A hundred clients. Some of them slow, some of them malicious, one of them a browser that opened a connection and then said nothing at all.

A normal read() on a socket blocks: if no data has arrived, your program stops there, doing nothing, until data comes. With one thread, one silent client freezes every other client on the server. That is why the naive version fails, and it is why the subject bans blocking.

This is a grade-zero rule, not a style preference

One poll() for everything. It must watch readable and writable at the same time. Never read or write a socket or pipe that poll() did not just report ready. Regular disk files are exempt in subject v24.1 — but the older evaluation sheet is stricter, and being stricter costs you nothing.
