the listGrouped by what they are for

Functions that do the same job with different spellings share a file — there is no reason to read about htons and htonl in two places.

  • 01 Making a socket exist The seven calls that turn nothing into a listening endpoint a browser can connect to. Run in this order, once per host:port in your config. socket · setsockopt · bind · listen · accept · getsockname · connect
  • 02 Moving bytes Four calls that read and write, and two that end a conversation. Every one of them may do less than you asked — that is the normal case, not an error. recv · send · read · write · close · shutdown
  • 03 Asking who is ready Pick exactly one of these and use it for everything. They all answer the same question — which of my descriptors can I touch right now without blocking — and differ only in how they scale and where they exist. poll · select · epoll_create · kqueue
  • 04 Byte order and addresses Small helpers with one job: making sure the number you wrote is the number the network reads. htons · ntohs · getaddrinfo · getprotobyname
  • 05 Running a CGI fork is banned in this project for everything except CGI. These five calls are the whole of that exception. fork · execve · waitpid · kill · signal
  • 06 Pipes and descriptor plumbing How the CGI child ends up reading the request body on its stdin and writing the page to its stdout without knowing anything about your server. pipe · dup2 · dup · fcntl · socketpair
  • 07 Reading the disk Turning a resolved path into a response — and answering the three questions that decide the status code: does it exist, what is it, and may I read it. stat · access · open · opendir · chdir
  • 08 Errors, and the one that can fail you Two harmless string helpers, and one macro that is on the allowed list yet will end your evaluation if you use it in the wrong place. errno · strerror · gai_strerror

before you readThree things true of the whole list

Being on the list is permission to link it, not permission to use it however you like. errno is the trap: allowed, and yet branching on it after a read or write ends the evaluation. Group 08 spells this out.

Almost every call here returns something you must check. The evaluation sheet calls out checking only -1 or only 0 as insufficient for the I/O calls — you check both, and act differently on each.

Prefer the C++ header spelling. <cstring> over <string.h>, <csignal> over <signal.h>. The subject asks for it explicitly, and it is free.
